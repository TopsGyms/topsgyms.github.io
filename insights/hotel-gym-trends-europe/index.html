Hotel Gym Trends in Europe: What Hotels Should Watch

The most useful hotel gym trends are not individual products. They are shifts in how properties think about fitness: more flexible training, stronger integration with the stay, clearer guidance and more deliberate investment.

By Sofie Tops — Founder, TopsGyms18 September 2026
Answer in briefWatch flexible strength and movement options, wellness integration, inclusive usability, digital support and lifecycle planning. Adopt only what fits the guest, property and operating model.

Trend 1: From equipment rooms to training environments

Hotels are increasingly treating the gym as an experience rather than a collection of machines. The practical implication is greater attention to zoning, open training space, lighting, storage and guidance. This does not require a large room, but it does require a coherent plan.

Properties should test whether flexible space genuinely supports guest needs. Removing useful equipment simply to create an open-looking room is not progress.

Trend 2: More visible strength training

Strength training is influencing guest expectations and equipment discussions. For hotels, the opportunity is to provide credible options across ability levels without creating an intimidating or operationally difficult environment.

Review movement patterns, adjustability, floor protection, noise and supervision requirements. A balanced strength offer is more important than following one product category.

Trend 3: Fitness and wellness working together

Guests do not always separate training, mobility, recovery and wellbeing into different departments. Hotels can create a clearer journey between these experiences through communication, scheduling, spatial relationships and service design.

Integration should remain honest. A gym does not become a wellness concept because recovery language is added to the wall. The offer, environment and operations need to support the promise.

Trend 4: Guidance without unnecessary complexity

Digital platforms, in-room content and on-site guidance can help guests understand the fitness offer. The best solution is the one the property can maintain and guests can access easily. Technology should remove uncertainty, not introduce another interface that quickly becomes outdated.

Simple orientation, clear equipment instructions and a small number of relevant workout suggestions may create more value than a large content system.

Trend 5: More inclusive usability

Hotel gyms serve a broad audience. Properties are paying more attention to equipment adjustment, circulation, confidence, language and different training abilities. Inclusive thinking improves the experience for many guests, not only one defined group.

Review access and usability with appropriate building, safety and accessibility expertise for the property and jurisdiction. A fitness strategy should surface these needs early enough to influence design.

Trend 6: Lifecycle and resource awareness

Replacing everything on a fixed visual cycle is not always the best decision. Hotels can benefit from clearer condition reviews, maintenance planning, selective replacement and adaptable layouts. This protects useful assets while keeping future improvement possible.

Claims about sustainability should be specific and supportable. The immediate strategic value is better stewardship of equipment, space and investment.

How to decide which trends matter

  • Does the idea answer a relevant guest need?
  • Does it support the property’s positioning?
  • Can the team operate and maintain it?
  • Does it improve training quality or reduce friction?
  • Will it remain useful beyond its novelty?

Use trends as inputs, not instructions. Compare each idea with the current gym, future plans and the six dimensions of the TopsGyms Gym Advancement Framework.

The durable direction

Across Europe, hotel contexts remain diverse. The strongest common direction is therefore not one standard gym. It is more deliberate decision-making: understand the guest, connect fitness to the property, preserve what works and improve what matters.

Trend 7: Stronger strategic briefs

Hotel fitness projects involve operations, wellness, interior design, engineering and equipment supply. A strategic brief helps these specialists work toward the same outcome. It should define the role of fitness, priority users, intended training possibilities and operating constraints before product decisions become fixed.

Trend 8: Personalisation through choice, not clutter

Properties want to serve different routines and confidence levels. The answer is not always more equipment. A balanced mix, adaptable zones and clear guidance can create choice while preserving order.

Be cautious with experiences that depend on extensive personal data, accounts or complex integrations. Digital personalisation needs appropriate privacy, security and operational review.

Turn monitoring into a process

Review developments at a defined interval rather than reacting to every launch. For each idea, record the guest need, expected benefit, operational requirement, space implication and evidence still needed. A trend matters only when the property can deliver it consistently.
